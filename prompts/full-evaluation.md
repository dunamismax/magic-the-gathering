## Required Audit

### 1. Deck Validation

Verify:

- Exactly 100 cards including the commander
- Singleton compliance, accounting for basic lands and cards that permit additional copies
- Commander color-identity compliance
- Current Commander legality
- Unreleased, previewed, silver-bordered, acorn, or Rule Zero cards
- Exact current Game Changer count and card names
- Any obvious decklist spelling or card-identification problems

If the list is not legal or is not exactly 100 cards, continue evaluating the submitted list but clearly flag the problem.

### 2. Strategy and Architecture

Identify:

- Primary strategy
- Secondary strategy
- Main engines
- Principal win conditions
- Commander’s role
- Whether the commander is essential, helpful, or largely optional
- How the deck normally develops during the early, middle, and late game
- Whether the deck is proactive, reactive, controlling, explosive, political, combat-focused, combo-focused, or a hybrid

Do not provide a lengthy card-by-card review.

### 3. Functional Composition

Count and assess the deck’s functional categories:

- Lands and land-capable cards
- Ramp and fast mana
- Cost reduction
- Cheap card selection
- Sustained card advantage
- Tutors
- Recursion
- Spot removal
- Countermagic
- Board wipes
- Protection
- Graveyard interaction
- Stack interaction
- Win conditions
- Dedicated combo pieces
- Flexible or modal cards

Cards may count in more than one category when appropriate. Clearly say when category counts overlap.

### 4. Combo and Closing-Line Audit

Identify every meaningful:

- Infinite combo
- Deterministic loop
- Unlimited or arbitrarily large line
- Compact two- or three-card win
- Finite but explosive synergy
- Soft lock, hard lock, or resource-denial pattern
- Repetitive tutor or recursion pattern

For each important line, state:

- Required cards
- Result
- Infinite, deterministic, finite, or merely synergistic
- Whether the commander is required
- Whether the pieces are useful independently
- How easily the line can be tutored or assembled
- Earliest plausible turn
- Most common disruption points

Do not describe a theoretical interaction as a reliable win unless the deck can realistically assemble and support it.

### 5. Speed Analysis

Estimate four separate timing windows:

- Earliest technically possible decisive turn
- Plausible strong-draw or “nut draw” turn
- Realistic uncontested closing window
- Realistic closing window against an appropriately matched interactive pod

A “decisive turn” may be a win, deterministic lock, overwhelming board state, or control position from which opponents are extremely unlikely to recover.

Distinguish theoretical ceiling from repeatable performance. Do not invent goldfish or simulation results. If actual simulations are performed, state the method and sample size.

### 6. Scorecard

Score each metric from 1–10, allowing half-points when useful. Explain every score in one concise sentence.

Evaluate:

- Overall power
- Power ceiling
- Speed
- Consistency
- Resilience
- Interaction
- Card advantage
- Mana efficiency
- Closing power
- Recovery after disruption
- Commander independence
- Replayability
- Decision complexity
- Threat perception
- Saltiness

Do not mechanically average these scores. Overall power should be a reasoned conclusion.

Keep power and salt separate: a deck may be powerful without being especially salty, or salty without being especially efficient.

For saltiness, specifically consider:

- Mass land destruction or mana denial
- Stax and hard locks
- Repeated extra turns
- Theft and control-changing effects
- Free interaction
- Heavy tutor density
- Early or repetitive infinite combos
- Commander shutdown
- Repeated board resets
- Graveyard locks
- Long solitaire turns
- Excessive trigger or bookkeeping load
- Highly repetitive play patterns
- A commander that attracts immediate table aggression

Salt is subjective, so identify the particular play patterns likely to create it rather than treating the score as universal.

### 7. Commander Bracket Determination

Assign the deck to the most appropriate current official Commander Bracket.

Explain:

- Why it belongs in that bracket
- Why it does not belong one bracket lower
- Whether it approaches or crosses into the next bracket
- Whether its construction and play intent agree
- Whether it can comfortably play up
- Whether it can responsibly play down
- Which features should be disclosed before a game

Do not determine the bracket from Game Changer count alone. Also consider speed, tutor density, fast mana, deterministic combos, extra turns, mass land denial, locks, resilience, interaction, and overall play intent.

If the deck sits between brackets, give one primary bracket and describe the lean, such as “upper Bracket 3, leaning toward Bracket 4.”

### 8. Strengths and Weaknesses

Provide:

- Three most important strengths
- Three most important weaknesses
- The deck’s most likely failure mode
- The types of opposing decks or interaction it handles well
- The types of opposing decks or interaction that expose it

### 9. Conservative Tuning Recommendations

Only include this section if tuning recommendations were requested.

Recommend no more than five high-confidence swaps. Suggest fewer when fewer are justified.

Rules:

- Do not recommend changes merely for variety or minor optimization.
- Preserve the deck’s stated identity and desired experience.
- Treat all stated constraints as hard requirements.
- Do not increase the Game Changer count unless explicitly authorized.
- Do not introduce an infinite combo when infinites are prohibited.
- Every addition must have a specific, meaningful purpose.
- Every cut must be clearly weaker, redundant, inefficient, unreliable, or poorly aligned.
- Additions and cuts must match exactly.
- If no sufficiently meaningful improvement exists, write “None” for both lists.

Use this exact format:

**Adds**
```text

1 Card Name
```

**Cuts**
```text
1 Card Name
1 Card Name
```

After the lists, explain only the most important swaps.

## Final Quality Rules

- Be candid and list-specific.
- Do not default every competent deck to “power level 7.”
- Avoid false precision and unsupported certainty.
- Separate theoretical ceiling from realistic consistency.
- Separate power from saltiness.
- Separate speed from resilience.
- Do not mistake high card prices for high power.
- Do not mistake zero Game Changers for a low-power deck.
- Do not mistake three Game Changers for automatic Bracket 3 eligibility.
- Do not pad the response with generic Commander advice.
- Keep the analysis comprehensive but clean, skimmable, and reasonably concise.
