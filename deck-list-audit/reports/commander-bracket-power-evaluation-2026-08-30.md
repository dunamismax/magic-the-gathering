# Commander bracket and power evaluation

Reviewed: 2026-08-30  
Profile: <https://moxfield.com/users/dunamismax>  
Scope: the exact 10 public lists and hashes recorded in
`data/moxfield-refresh.json`

## Verdict

The collection contains one Bracket 2 deck and nine Bracket 3 decks. None is a
Bracket 1, Bracket 4, or Bracket 5 build as currently constructed. Pantlaza is
the only knife-edge decision: its overall intent and normal clock fit the top
of Bracket 3, but its tutored conditional kill should be disclosed, and a pilot
who actively seeks that line should present the deck as Bracket 4.

The numerical ranges are conventional relative power estimates, not a formula
that converts directly into brackets. Brackets classify expected game
experience and intent. The ranges distinguish decks that share the very wide
Bracket 3 space without pretending that a singleton multiplayer deck has a
stable decimal score.

These are list-based estimates, not measured win rates. No game logs or
goldfish results were supplied, so normal-clock and consistency statements are
inferences from the current mana, engines, tutors, interaction, and finishers.
Repeated pre-turn-seven wins would be evidence to move a proposed B3 label to
B4 regardless of whether the win is combat or a combo.

## Why these brackets fit

### Pantlaza, Sun-Favored — ceiling Bracket 3

Pantlaza has the collection's highest ceiling: strong mana, 15 cards classified
as ramp, five protection pieces, broad creature interaction, repeated discover,
and multiple creature tutors. The exact list also contains three confirmed
`Apex Altisaur` + `Wrathful Raptors` lines. `Savage Order` is the sharp version
because four mana both finds Apex and makes it indestructible. With a suitable
opposing creature, the line can produce unbounded damage; without one, it is
usually a one-sided creature clear with substantial damage.

The deck still fits Bracket 3 by normal intent: it is a high-curve Dinosaur
combat/value deck, has only `Sol Ring` as fast mana, has zero Game Changers, and
does not consistently threaten the turn-four-or-five optimized clock expected
of Bracket 4. That judgment changes if the pilot routinely assembles the
Raptors line or mulligans and tutors toward it. For blind matchmaking without a
pregame disclosure, Bracket 4 is the safer seat.

### Magda, Brazen Outlaw — upper Bracket 3

Magda is the most consistent engine in the command zone. Cheap Dwarves and tap
outlets make Treasures, and every five Treasures become the best Dragon or
artifact for the position. The deck can snowball into `Maskwood Nexus`,
`Roaming Throne`, `Terror of the Peaks`, or a large Dragon turn without drawing
a conventional tutor.

It remains Bracket 3 because the current 100 deliberately omits the compact
untap and artifact loops that turn Magda into an optimized combo commander. The
tutor chains stop, spot interaction and protection are limited, and no exact
list combo was found. Disclose Magda's repeatable command-zone tutor even though
there is no infinite line.

### Kang Prime — upper Bracket 3

Kang has a powerful late-game engine: top-deck setup converts high-mana cards
into suspended free spells, while blink, clones, trigger doublers, and added
beginning phases multiply or accelerate those spells. The best turns can deploy
`Omniscience`, Eldrazi, `Galactus, Devourer of Worlds`, or reanimation payloads
for little or no mana. It also has a solid mana-rock package and meaningful
instant-speed interaction.

The five-mana commander, two-counter delay, average nonland mana value above
four, and commander dependence keep the typical clock in Bracket 3. The current
list contains no extra-turn spell, `Displacer Kitten`, Blightsteel line, or
deterministic loop. Its explosive ceiling is high, but it is not built to end
games as fast or consistently as a Bracket 4 deck.

### Henzie “Toolbox” Torre — mid-upper Bracket 3

Henzie combines early mana creatures with discounted blitz threats, premium
ETBs, death value, sacrifice outlets, `Birthing Pod`, and mass reanimation.
Unlike several linear decks in the collection, it can rebuild well and play
through creature removal. Its common strong turn is a large blitz or
reanimation sequence after the table has developed, which matches Bracket 3's
one-big-turn expectation.

The Spellbook persist template is incomplete because the current list has no
persist creature. There is no compact win, little stack protection, and the
deck normally wins through creatures, ETBs, or combat rather than an
instantaneous optimized finish.

### Minn, Wily Illusionist — mid-upper Bracket 3

Minn has the deepest card-advantage engine in the lineup. Second-draw payoffs,
draw multipliers, clones, sacrifice outlets, and Minn's death trigger turn
Illusions into cards, mana, damage, and free permanents. Its strongest boards
can compound quickly and generate much more value than an ordinary Core deck.

Its current weaknesses matter: interaction is light, there is no counterspell
suite or board wipe, ramp is modest, and the three sacrifice outlets consume a
finite supply of Illusions. The refreshed exact-list scan and manual review
found no loop. That makes it a strong value-oriented Bracket 3 deck rather than
an optimized combo deck.

### Blor the Impervious — mid-upper Bracket 3

Blor is unusually difficult to answer because the commander naturally has
trample, hexproof, and indestructible. One-mana acceleration and a low average
mana value help cast Blor early, while double strike, power doublers, and combat
draw can remove a player suddenly. That gives the deck a higher single-player
kill ceiling than its zero-combo construction might suggest.

The plan is still visible, combat-based, and mostly one player at a time. It
has no deterministic combo and must invest cards and combat steps into its
commander. This is upper social play, not an optimized table-kill deck. `Blor
the Impervious` is unreleased until 2026-11-09, so it requires Rule Zero before
then; that legality issue does not reduce its power bracket.

### Ekthi, Contaminator Priest — middle Bracket 3

Ekthi turns every Equipment into a living-weapon threat and combines that with
five Equipment tutors, free or cheap equip effects, strong recursion, and
high-impact equipment such as `Colossus Hammer` and `Kaldra Compleat`. Poison
cards create a real alternate combat clock.

The deck remains commander-dependent and board-dependent. Its wins are finite,
telegraphed, and vulnerable to artifact or creature sweeps, and the exact-list
scan found no combo. It has the card quality and synergy of Bracket 3 without
the consistency or speed of Bracket 4. `Ekthi, Contaminator Priest` is
unreleased until 2026-11-09 and currently requires Rule Zero.

### Queen Marchesa — middle Bracket 3

Queen Marchesa has the collection's strongest reactive posture: 24 instants,
11 spot-removal effects by the local role classifier, goad, damage redirection,
and political card flow. `Sunforger` and `Wishclaw Talisman` add access, but the
deck usually wins by steering combat and converting opponents' damage rather
than racing.

The exact list has a finite `Fire Covenant` + `Brash Taunter` kill and a real
five-card `Arcbond` loop. The latter is too expensive and card-intensive to make
the deck Bracket 4, but it should be disclosed. Slow closing speed and reliance
on table state keep raw power near the middle of Bracket 3 even though the deck
can exert strong control over a game.

### Marrow-Gnawer — lower-middle Bracket 3

Marrow-Gnawer is focused and can scale frighteningly through 26 `Rat Colony`,
copied Rat production, tribal lords, drain effects, and mass recursion. It is
well above an unoptimized Core deck once Marrow survives a turn cycle.

The current list lacks `Thrumming Stone`, `Thornbite Staff`, and the compact
combo/tutor package that previously made the archetype much sharper. It has
only four ramp cards in the local classification, little protection, and light
interaction. Its creature-based wins are visible and disruptable. Those facts
place it safely in Bracket 3, toward the lower end of this collection's nine B3
decks.

### The Fifth Doctor + Susan Foreman — upper Bracket 2

This deck creates incremental value by tapping and untapping creatures,
distributing +1/+1 counters, and crewing Vehicles and Spacecraft. It has useful
synergy and a high-quality mana base, but it omits `Sol Ring`, has no Game
Changer or complete combo, and normally needs several permanents and combat
steps to close. Its Helix effects and `Basking Broodscale` do not have the
missing loop pieces.

The resulting gameplay is proactive, telegraphed, and usually gives opponents
at least eight turns, which is the current Core expectation. It is a good upper
Bracket 2 deck and would be at a disadvantage in most of this collection's B3
pods.

## Collection-wide facts and caveats

- All 10 exact lists contain 100 cards and pass the local singleton, color
  identity, legality-policy, banned-card, and configured Game Changer checks,
  including the declared Rule Zero exceptions.
- All 10 contain zero current Game Changers. That does not determine bracket;
  Magda's command-zone tutor and Pantlaza's combo are the clearest examples.
- No list contains a true extra-turn spell or mass land denial package.
- Pantlaza has three confirmed conditional near-infinite lines. Queen Marchesa
  has one finite kill and one five-card unbounded loop. Henzie's returned
  persist template is incomplete. The other seven lists have no known complete
  exact-list combo after the 2026-08-30 Spellbook and manual review.
- Rule Zero is presently required for `Blor the Impervious`, `Ekthi,
  Contaminator Priest`, `The Theorist, Jace Beleren` in Minn, and `Maular, the
  Next Evolution` in Pantlaza because those cards have future release dates.
- The power ordering measures expected performance. The separate side-eye
  ordering in `commander-social-audit-2026-08-30.md` measures blind-pod concern,
  so political control, hidden loops, or hard-to-answer commanders can rank
  differently there.

## Evidence and limits

This evaluation uses the exact deck hashes in `data/moxfield-refresh.json`, the
2026-08-30 Oracle audit, the current official Commander guidance, the full-list
Commander Spellbook results refreshed on 2026-08-30, and the manual prerequisite
review in `knowledge/combo-adjudications.json`.

The official framework emphasizes intent and expected turns: Core is
unoptimized, incremental, and generally allows at least eight turns; Upgraded
has strong synergy and interaction and generally allows at least six turns;
Optimized is fast, lethal, consistent, and can reasonably end a player's game
after four turns. Commander Spellbook is incomplete, and no-combo findings are
limited to its current database plus the documented manual screen.
